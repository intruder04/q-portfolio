Losses you could use

Shares that are worth less than you paid. Selling them turns the loss into a deduction: it offsets gains, then up to $3,000 of other income a year, and the rest carries forward. Tax it could offset is the most it's worth at your rates. A loss is blocked (a wash sale) if you bought the same stock within 30 days before or after selling.

How this is worked out

  • Wash sale checks look only at this account's purchases of the same stock. Purchases in your other accounts (retirement accounts and a spouse's included), and of nearly identical funds, count too.
  • A share is long-term when it's sold after the one-year anniversary of its purchase. Long-term from is the first day that applies.
  • When you sell by dollar amount, your broker picks the shares. Some sell losing shares first, then the shares with the smallest tax (usually long-term gains); others sell the oldest first, the IRS default. Your long-term shares are your oldest, so selling up to their value creates no short-term gains under either rule. After a sale, your broker's cost basis details show which shares it used.
  • Sales by tax year come from the sales in your export, with each sale's shares split into short- and long-term. Dividends, other accounts and losses carried over from earlier years aren't included.
  • Tax estimates use the rates at the top: long-term gains at your long-term rate, short-term gains at your income tax bracket, plus your state rate and the 3.8% tax if you ticked it. A loss of one kind offsets a gain of the other. Deductions, limits and your other gains and losses for the year aren't counted.
  • Current prices and all-time highs come from Yahoo Finance when this report was made. The all-time high is the highest intraday price ever, adjusted for splits. The verdicts use simple cutoffs; being near a high doesn't predict where a price goes next.
  • This is a holding-period and tax estimate, not tax or investment advice.
